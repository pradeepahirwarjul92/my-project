1. What Agile methodologies have you worked with?

Answer:
"I have primarily worked in Scrum-based Agile teams and have also been involved in Kanban for production support activities. In Scrum, 
we followed sprint planning, daily stand-ups, backlog refinement, 
sprint reviews, and retrospectives. Typically, our sprint duration was two weeks."


2. What is the role of a Scrum Master?

Answer:

Facilitates Scrum ceremonies.
Removes impediments.
Protects the team from external distractions.
Ensures Agile principles are followed.
Helps improve team productivity.


3. What is the difference between Scrum and Kanban?
| Scrum                 | Kanban              |
| --------------------- | ------------------- |
| Fixed sprint duration | Continuous flow     |
| Sprint backlog        | No sprint backlog   |
| Velocity tracking     | Lead time tracking  |
| Defined ceremonies    | Fewer ceremonies    |
| Time-boxed delivery   | Continuous delivery |


4. How do you estimate stories?

Answer:
"We use Planning Poker and Story Points. Estimation considers complexity, 
effort, dependencies, risks, and uncertainty rather than actual hours."

Example:

1 Point → Simple API change
3 Points → CRUD module
5 Points → Complex business logic
8+ Points → Multiple integrations

5. What are Story Points?

Answer:
Story Points measure relative effort considering:

Complexity
Risk
Effort
Unknowns

They are not equal to hours.


6. What is Velocity?

Answer:
Velocity is the number of Story Points completed in a sprint.

Example:

Sprint 1 = 40 SP
Sprint 2 = 42 SP
Sprint 3 = 38 SP

Average Velocity = 40 SP

Used for release planning.


7. What happens during Sprint Planning?

Answer:

Product Owner presents prioritized stories.
Team discusses requirements.
Team estimates effort.
Sprint goal is defined.
Stories are committed based on capacity.


8. What do you discuss in Daily Stand-up?

Three questions:

What did I complete yesterday?
What will I do today?
Are there any blockers?

Keep it within 15 minutes.


9. What is Backlog Refinement?

Answer:
Process of:

Clarifying requirements.
Breaking large stories into smaller ones.
Estimating stories.
Prioritizing work.

Ensures stories are ready for the next sprint.


10. What is Definition of Done (DoD)?

Answer:
A story is done only when:

Development completed.
Code review completed.
Unit tests passed.
Integration tests passed.
Sonar issues resolved.
Documentation updated.
Deployed to QA/UAT.


11. What is Definition of Ready (DoR)?

Answer:
A story is ready when:

Requirements are clear.
Acceptance criteria exist.
Dependencies identified.
Estimation completed.



12. How do you handle changing requirements during a sprint?

Answer (10-year level):
"Normally we avoid scope changes during an active sprint. If the change is critical, Product Owner discusses impact with the team and 
may replace a lower-priority story or move the change to the next sprint."



13. What do you do when a story cannot be completed within the sprint?

Answer:

Inform Scrum Master and Product Owner early.
Explain blockers.
Re-estimate remaining work.
Move incomplete work back to the backlog.


14. What is a Sprint Retrospective?

Answer:
Meeting to discuss:

What went well.
What didn't go well.
Action items for improvement.

Example:

Improve code review turnaround time.
Reduce deployment failures.
Increase automation coverage.

16. How do you handle production issues in Agile?

Answer:
"We maintain a separate support lane. Critical P1/P2 issues are handled immediately, while normal 
defects are prioritized in the product backlog. We also perform RCA and create preventive action items."

17. Explain a conflict between Product Owner and Development Team.

Answer:
"PO wanted additional features in the current sprint, but the team had already committed to sprint goals. I explained the impact on delivery commitments and proposed 
prioritizing the changes for the next sprint. This maintained transparency and trust.


18. How do you manage dependencies across teams?

Answer:

Early identification.
Cross-team refinement meetings.
Dependency tracking in Jira.
Regular communication.
Escalation when blockers impact delivery.


19. What is your role as a senior developer in Agile?

Answer:

Technical design discussions.
Story estimation.
Mentoring junior developers.
Code reviews.
Production support.
Risk identification.
Collaboration with PO, QA, DevOps.


20. Have you participated in release planning?

Answer:
"Yes. Based on team velocity, backlog size, dependencies, and resource availability, we forecast release timelines and identify risks early."


21. Sprint is ending but only 60% work is completed. What will you do?

Answer:

Discuss status transparently.
Identify blockers.
Deliver completed stories only.
Move remaining work back to backlog.
Analyze root cause during retrospective.


22. Team velocity suddenly drops. How do you investigate?

Answer:

Check production support load.
Resource availability.
Dependency delays.
Requirement changes.
Technical challenges.


23. Product Owner wants an urgent feature in the middle of the sprint.

Answer:

Assess impact.
Discuss trade-offs.
Replace lower-priority story if necessary.
Otherwise schedule for next sprint.


24. How do you improve Agile maturity?

Answer:

Better backlog grooming.
Strong Definition of Done.
CI/CD automation.
Automated testing.
Regular retrospectives.
Knowledge sharing sessions.


25. Why Agile instead of Waterfall?"

| Agile                | Waterfall           |
| -------------------- | ------------------- |
| Iterative delivery   | Sequential delivery |
| Faster feedback      | Late feedback       |
| Flexible changes     | Difficult changes   |
| Continuous testing   | Testing at end      |
| Customer involvement | Limited involvement |
